# Budget Framework

## Step 1: Ceiling and priorities

Ask for the total ceiling and top three priorities. Without a ceiling there is no overspend, only a bigger number later. In the real plan this was derived from, no ceiling existed, so variance could not be measured.

## Step 2: Allocation (starting point, adjust to priorities)

These shares are **rules of thumb to start the conversation, not data**. Use them only to propose a first draft, then adjust.

| Category | Typical share (guide) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Venue and food/beverage | 40-55% | Usually the biggest line. Ask whether quotes include service charge and tax. |
| Rings, jewellery, gold | 5-20% | Varies hugely by culture; log each purchase. |
| Attire, hair, makeup | 5-10% | Alterations and add-ons usually cost more than the quote. |
| Photo/video | 5-12% | Book early; confirm delivery timeline. |
| Decor, flowers, stationery, favours | 5-10% | Set a vendor cap before collecting quotes. |
| Family customs (tea ceremony, betrothal gifts, ang bao) | 3-8% | Often forgotten at the start. |
| Transport, accommodation, misc | 2-5% | |
| Tips, helper gifts, ang bao for helpers | 1-3% | Real example: about 2% of total spend, logged 3 days after the wedding. |
| **Contingency** | **5-10%** | Mandatory. |

One real-world split from the source plan (roughly 100 guests, outdoor morning ceremony plus lunch): venue and food ~52%, jewellery ~22%, engagement ~7.5%, other ~5.4%, attire ~5.2%, betrothal gifts ~3.9%, photoshoot ~3.8%. Your split will differ with your priorities, culture and guest count.

## Step 3: Ledger design (do this, not a single list of expenses)

**Vendors table:** name, category, status (Shortlisted, Quoted, Booked, Paid in full), contact, quote total, contract link, day-of arrival time.

**Payments table** (one row per payment, linked to vendor): vendor, label (deposit / balance / extra), amount, due date, paid date, payer, status (Planned / Due / Paid), receipt link.

**Budget view:** per category show Planned, Committed (sum of signed quotes), Paid, Remaining = Planned - Committed.

Rule: every row has a status. A blank status is a bug (in the source plan, about 6% of spend had none).

## Step 4: Quote vs actual

Record the quote when you sign. Re-record when add-ons are agreed. Show variance. Example from the source plan: the gown cost about 11% more than quoted.

## Step 5: Monthly reconciliation (15 minutes)

1. Export or list bank/card transactions tagged to the wedding.
2. Match each to a Payment row; mark Paid with date.
3. Flag any figure that appears in two places with different numbers (the source plan had a gap worth about 12% of total spend between a vendor page and its ledger row).
4. List payments due in the next 30 days.
5. Update Remaining by category and say which category is closest to its cap.

## Cash flow shape (typical)

Large deposits land in the first 3 months (venue, ring, photographer), mid-size items in months 4-7 (attire, florist, favours, bands), balances 4-6 weeks before, and a long tail of small purchases in the last 6 weeks. The tail is where untracked spend creeps in: set a "small purchases" envelope and log every receipt.

## Questions to ask vendors before signing

- What is included/excluded? Service charge, GST/VAT, overtime, corkage, setup/teardown time?
- Deposit amount, balance due date, cancellation and date-change terms?
- What is the latest we can change guest numbers or menu?
- Venue: earliest load-in time and exact clear-out time? Rain backup space?
